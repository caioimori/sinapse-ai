# Business Storytelling Frameworks

> Version: 1.0.0 | Agents: kindra-hall, park-howell | Domain: brand-business, sales-persuasion

## Overview

Business storytelling is the strategic use of narrative to achieve commercial outcomes: sales, trust, culture, investment, change. It differs from literary storytelling in that the goal is always specific, measurable action — not artistic experience.

The two primary specialists: **Kindra Hall** (stories that stick in business) and **Park Howell** (brand storytelling strategy).

---

## Kindra Hall — 4 Stories Framework

**Source:** "Stories That Stick" (2019)

Kindra Hall argues that most business communication suffers from "Story Gaps" — places where a story SHOULD exist but doesn't. The result: facts, data, and features fail to connect.

### The 4 Essential Business Stories

#### 1. The Value Story
**Purpose:** Communicate product/service value in a way that logic alone cannot.
**When to use:** Sales conversations, product demos, marketing campaigns
**Structure:** A customer in their specific situation → the specific obstacle they faced → the specific moment your solution made a difference → the measurable outcome

**The mistake:** Talking about features and benefits instead of telling the story of someone who experienced those benefits.

**Example structure:**
```
[Customer name/type] was dealing with [specific situation].
Every time [specific trigger], [specific painful consequence].
Until they tried [your product/approach].
Now [specific changed reality] — [measurable result].
```

#### 2. The Founder Story
**Purpose:** Build trust and credibility through origin narrative.
**When to use:** Sales calls, investor pitches, brand communications, recruiting
**Structure:** Who the founder was BEFORE → the specific moment of realization → the decision to build → the transformation in understanding

**Why it works:** Humans trust humans. Institutional communication creates distance. The founder's story creates identification — "this person faced a real problem that I recognize."

**Key elements:**
- Specific, not generic (a real moment, not "we saw a market opportunity")
- Vulnerability (what they didn't know, what they got wrong)
- The pivot point (the moment the course was set)
- Connection to customer's problem

#### 3. The Purpose Story
**Purpose:** Galvanize teams around a shared mission; answer "why does this work matter?"
**When to use:** All-hands meetings, culture building, onboarding, change management
**Structure:** The problem in the world → why it matters beyond the commercial → who suffers from it → why THIS team is positioned to solve it

**Why it works:** Simon Sinek's "Why" made conceptual — but the purpose story makes it narrative. A stated purpose is easy to forget; a purpose embodied in a story stays.

**Caution:** Purpose stories must be true. Teams detect inauthenticity instantly.

#### 4. The Customer Story
**Purpose:** Social proof through narrative (not just testimonial bullets).
**When to use:** Marketing campaigns, case studies, sales collateral, website
**Structure:** Who the customer was → their specific challenge → their decision to try your solution → the transformation → their new reality

**The difference from testimonials:** Testimonials are statements ("This product is great!"). Customer stories have structure, specificity, and tension. The tension (the "explosion") is what makes them credible.

## Hall's Story Structure: Normal → Explosion → New Normal

Every one of Hall's 4 stories follows this three-part pattern:

```
NORMAL:      The character's ordinary reality before the story
EXPLOSION:   The moment everything changed — the disruption
NEW NORMAL:  The transformed reality after the story
```

**The Explosion is the story.** Everything before it is setup; everything after it is payoff. The explosion must be:
- Specific (not "things got better" — an actual moment)
- Surprising (creates cognitive engagement)
- Consequential (something real was at stake)

### Story Ingredients (Hall's 4)

1. **Identifiable characters** — Real people with specific names, roles, contexts
2. **Authentic emotion** — Not manufactured sentiment; actual feeling at stake
3. **A significant moment** — The explosion must feel pivotal, not gradual
4. **Specific details** — Time, place, sensory details that create vividness

## Park Howell — ABT Framework

**Source:** "Brand Bewitchery" + Randy Olson's "Houston, We Have a Narrative" (2015)

### And-But-Therefore

The most compact and powerful narrative structure ever created. Can diagnose and fix any communication in 30 seconds.

```
[AND]: Context, setup, agreement — the status quo
[BUT]: Conflict, tension, disruption — the problem
[THEREFORE]: Resolution, action, consequence — the response
```

**The AAA anti-pattern:** Most business communication suffers from "And-And-And" — a list of facts with no tension:
> "We have 10 years of experience AND we serve Fortune 500 clients AND we have 99% uptime AND our NPS is 72..."

**The ABT fix:**
> "We have 10 years of experience serving Fortune 500 clients, AND our competitors do too. BUT our clients consistently leave them and come to us after experiencing the difference. THEREFORE we've built a switching program that makes migration seamless."

**ABT as diagnostic:**
Run any message through ABT. If you can't find the "But," there's no story — just information. The "But" is where narrative tension lives.

## Park Howell — Story Cycle System (10 Steps)

**Source:** "Brand Bewitchery"

A complete brand storytelling strategy framework that runs the full hero's journey from the brand's perspective:

| Step | Name | Description |
|------|------|-------------|
| 1 | **Backstory** | The world before your brand existed — the problem landscape |
| 2 | **Desire** | What your customer yearns for (not what your product does) |
| 3 | **Resistance** | The obstacles, doubts, and fears blocking the desire |
| 4 | **Adventure** | The brand's solution — the path through resistance |
| 5 | **Allies & Enemies** | Forces that help or hinder your customer |
| 6 | **Trial** | The decisive moment — the test of the solution |
| 7 | **Death** | The old way, the old self, must die |
| 8 | **Rebirth** | Transformation — the customer is different now |
| 9 | **Return** | The customer re-enters the world, changed |
| 10 | **Encore** | The customer's ongoing story — they become advocates |

**The key principle:** The customer is the hero. The brand is the guide, mentor, and ally — never the protagonist.

## Donald Miller — StoryBrand (SB7 Framework)

**Source:** "Building a StoryBrand" (2017)

StoryBrand is the business storytelling framework most used in marketing and website copy. It applies the hero's journey structure specifically to brand communication.

### The 7 Elements of StoryBrand

| Element | Description | Example |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| **A Character** | The customer is the hero — define them precisely | "Entrepreneurs who want to grow" |
| **Has a Problem** | External + Internal + Philosophical problems | External: losing customers / Internal: feeling incompetent / Philosophical: good businesses should thrive |
| **Meets a Guide** | The brand as mentor — empathy + authority | "We've helped 10,000 businesses..." |
| **Who Gives Them a Plan** | Clear 3-step process that makes action simple | "1. Schedule call. 2. Get plan. 3. Grow." |
| **And Calls Them to Action** | Direct and transitional CTAs | "Get Started" + "Learn More" |
| **That Helps Them Avoid Failure** | Stakes — what happens if they don't act | "Don't lose another customer to a confusing website" |
| **And Ends in Success** | The vision of what their life looks like after | "Customers who find you. Revenue that grows." |

**Why it works:** It positions the brand as a guide (Obi-Wan, not Luke), which is counterintuitive for most brands who want to be the hero of their own story. The customer is Luke. Your product is the lightsaber.

## Story Gap Analysis (Hall)

Hall's concept: businesses consistently fail to tell stories at the moments that matter most. The Story Gap is the space where a story SHOULD exist but doesn't.

**Common Story Gaps:**

| Moment | What Usually Happens | What Should Happen |
|--------|---------------------|-------------------|
| First sales meeting | Product demo with features | Customer story that mirrors prospect's situation |
| Website homepage | Value proposition bullets | Founder story or customer story |
| Investor pitch | Financial projections | Purpose story + customer story |
| Team meeting | Metrics and updates | Purpose story reminding why the work matters |
| Onboarding | Process documentation | Founder story + what we stand for |

## Comparing Business Story Frameworks

| Framework | Scale | Primary Use | Best Strength |
|-----------|-------|-------------|---------------|
| Kindra Hall's 4 Stories | Micro-Meso | Sales, marketing, culture | Identifying WHICH story to tell |
| ABT | Micro | Any communication | Rapid diagnosis and repair |
| Story Cycle (Howell) | Macro | Complete brand narrative | Full-system brand storytelling |
| StoryBrand (Miller) | Meso | Website, marketing copy | Clear customer-hero positioning |
| Story Gap Analysis | Any | Communication audit | Finding where stories are missing |

## The Cialdini Connection — Persuasion Science in Business Stories

Robert Cialdini's 7 principles of influence map onto business storytelling:

| Cialdini Principle | Story Application |
|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Reciprocity** | Give real value through stories before asking for anything |
| **Commitment** | Stories that let prospects say "yes, that's me" in small ways |
| **Social Proof** | Customer stories ARE the most powerful social proof |
| **Authority** | Founder story and expertise narrative build authority |
| **Liking** | Vulnerability and humor in stories create affinity |
| **Scarcity** | The consequence story shows what's lost without acting |
| **Unity** | Purpose stories create "us" — shared identity and values |
